# 07_PROBLEM_GENERATION — Drill Composition, Weak-Spot Targeting, Progression & AI-Written Problem Verification

**Module Code:** `RULES-PROBLEM-GEN`
**Applies To:** Deciding which drills to author, and verifying AI-written problems and variants
**Prerequisite:** `rules/00_core.md`, `rules/02_pedagogy.md`, `rules/06_quality_contract.md`
**Does not redefine:** levels L1–L5, trap provenance, gates G1–G6, reporting (see 06 §2, §3, §4, §8)
**Labels:** `[code]` = enforced by tooling today. `[manual]` = procedure for the agent or learner; no tooling yet.

## 1. Scope and composition
- `[code]` A drill's scope is one unit (unit-only drill), one chapter (mixed over its units), or cross-unit bridge synthesis (`MTH-BRIDGE`, `math_engine/reasoning_vault/MTH-BRIDGE.json`); see §4 "Scope and levels". Bridge archetypes combine skills from ≥2 passed units (mastery ≥ 2 per `scripts/bridge_scope.py`) at L3+ only, remain dormant until ≥2 units qualify, and require every referenced unit to be tagged on at least one step (`steps[].unit`).
- `[manual]` Drills are AI-authored from reasoning vaults via `new-math-drill` (`math_engine.ai_drill_ingest`) and `new-code-drill` (`code_engine.ai_drill_ingest`). Requested difficulty takes one level (1–5) or `ALL`; never silently fall back to a lower level (06 §2.2).
- Composition follows the Unit Practice Guide (02 §8). A unit-review drill stays within its Unit; chapter drills mix that chapter's Units, and eligible bridge drills intentionally combine two or more passed Units. `[manual]` A study session may use:
  1. Core drill: L1 or L2 (or `ALL`), including at least one reverse form.
  2. Trap drill: L3, or a weak-spot drill (section 2).
  3. Prior-unit review: use a separate drill for an earlier Unit when doing unit-level review; chapter and bridge scopes follow the rules above.
  4. Applied drill: L4 when the Unit has L4-capable archetypes.
  5. Synthesis drill: L5 for cross-topic interleaving and non-routine problem solving.
- Never duplicate problems to pad a drill set (06 §2.2, G3).

## 2. Weak-spot targeting
- Post-drill mistake summaries count checker-added tags from the current cycle and retain learner-selected tags. Historical attempts remain available as evidence.
- Sources, most to least reliable: (1) mistake tags with `trapMatched` in >= 2 attempts, (2) `concept-gap` skips, (3) the learner's Mistake & Pitfall Log (02 §7), (4) learner statement. `unclassified` wrong answers are data to inspect, not a weakness label. `durationMs` and `hintsRevealed` are supporting signals only.
- `[code]` Counting tags: `python -m scripts.weak_spots [SCOPE]` joins learner progress (`progress/`) with drill content and reports, per unit, open weak spots (`matchedTrap` >= 2), closed ones (the last 2 drills that tested the trap were clean and unassisted), level advice from the last 5 items, assisted and skipped items, and the next weak-spot drill to make. A weak-spot drill sets `focusTags`.
- `[code]` A weak-spot drill targets 1–2 trap tags. Author via `new-math-drill` (or `new-code-drill`) selecting archetypes whose `cognitiveTraps` include the tag and setting `focusTags`. Prefer 1–2 tags per drill and include a reverse form where appropriate.
- `[code]` A weak spot closes after 2 consecutive weak-spot drills with all items correct; any `trapMatched` for that tag reopens it (`scripts/weak_spots.py`). With no data, run the core and trap drills and collect data first.

## 3. Progression
- `[code]` Starting level advice and progression signals come from `python -m scripts.weak_spots [SCOPE]` / `scripts/weak_spots.py`: the last 5 items, score thresholds, reverse coverage, and PRG `debugging` / `refactoring` variety with `QC-*` and lint signals. These are recommendations for the next drill, not automatic curriculum changes.
- `[code]` Post-drill coaching (`python -m scripts.drill_review <DRILL_ID> [--json]`, `.claude/skills/review-drill/SKILL.md`): read-only, zero-leak diagnostic joining `progress_store.score_problem` / `cycle_evidence`, `_review_schedule.json`, `weak_spots`, `vault_report`, and `math_engine.variant_bank`. Computes one deterministic `primary` recommendation plus at most two `alternatives` (each with `action`, `why`, and `params` ready for `new-math-drill`): review due within 2 days → do before new work; skipped items → unit concept recap + lowest level of the archetype; repeated trap across drills → unit trap-contrast recap + trap-focused drill; wrong/assisted items → unattempted Similar retry first, then targeted review drill (or unassisted fluency consolidation after heavy help); all clean at max level ≤ 2 or Mastery-capped → L3–L4 drill on unpractised/high archetypes to satisfy the cap reason; all clean through L4+ uncapped with no open weak spots → advance to next unit.
- Difficulty comes from reasoning, not arithmetic size.

Claude only reviews `scripts.source_gap <UNIT>` output (against `math_engine/reasoning_vault/` for MTH — including uncovered chain cards and the `"no L2 chain archetype"` warning using exact normalised operation equality and `level` = 2, with optional `operations` of ≥ 2 distinct normalised items on math vault archetypes accepted by `code_engine.quality_checks --check-vaults` — or `code_engine/reasoning_vault/` `cognitiveTraps`/`qualityChecks` for PRG) for vault updates and reviewer verification. Every problem and lab must be original: do not copy, translate, or closely paraphrase source exercises, or paste/rename source code. `math_engine.source_cores.originality` (and `code_originality` token-level identifier/whitespace-normalised Winnowing/k-gram fingerprints + prompt text checks for PRG) enforces originality and is wired into `math_engine.ai_drill_ingest --check`, `math_engine.variant_bank --check`, `code_engine.ai_drill_ingest --check`, and `code_engine.lab_ingest --check` (`--originality`, default ON when `sources/` exists). OpenStax is scope-only; do not bulk-download or scrape it. PRG Similar/variants are not built yet (math only).
- `[code]` **Reasoning Vault & Gap Analysis**:
  - **Math Vault (`math_engine/reasoning_vault/<unit_code>.json`)**: Novel mathematical structures are extracted into mental models, governing relations, and cognitive traps.
  - **Code Vault (`code_engine/reasoning_vault/<unit_code>.json`)**: Software engineering patterns are recorded with `archetypeKey`, `level`, `coreConcept`, `mentalModel`, `cognitiveTraps` (Bug/Smell Patterns with reproducers), `realWorldDomains`, and `usedContexts`.
  - When a problem does use a story (§4 item 8), the AI rotates contexts across domains and updates `usedContexts` to prevent repetition.
- `[code]` **Mandatory AI Contract Guard for Math (`validate_ai_problem_contract`)**:
  1. **Typed Givens**: Every numeric literal appearing in `prompt` (excluding standard year tokens) must map directly to an entry in `givens` or `constants`, and every entry in `givens` must appear in `prompt` (exponents such as the `3` in `$2^3$` count as appearing).
     - **`constants`** holds numbers that belong to the method, not the story: formula constants (the `2` in $P = 2L + 2W$, the $\frac{1}{3}$ in a cone volume, $90$ for complementary angles, $180$ for a triangle) and values the learner must derive. They never have to appear in `prompt`. A key may not be in both `givens` and `constants`.
     - Never mention a number in `prompt` only to satisfy this rule. If a number is part of the method, put it in `constants`.
  2. **Zero-Number Hint (00_core §6)**: `checkpointHint` must contain strictly ZERO numbers from the problem. It must express only abstract mathematical relationships, formulas, or self-monitoring process verification steps (06 §3.4).
  3. **Trap Derivation & Choice Distractor Provenance (06 §3, §4 G4; `validate_distractor_provenance`)**: `trapDerivation` must be an operational expression referencing at least 2 distinct values from `givens` or `constants` (misusing a formula constant, e.g. $2 + r$ instead of $2r$, is a valid trap), evaluate with SymPy from `givens`/`constants` to `trapValue`, use at least `min(2, len(trapGivens))` values from `trapGivens` when declared, match the vault's `trapFormula` when the archetype defines a SymPy-evaluable formula over `givens`, and satisfy `check_answer(trapValue) == incorrect` with `trapMatched: True` (and differ from `verify.source`). A single-given `interval` problem may use one given; an `is_omission_trap` is exempt from the two-given requirement. On `choice` problems:
     - Every distractor option must carry a vault `trapTag` from the problem archetype's `cognitiveTraps` and a non-empty `provenance` (or `trapFormula` / `derivation`; for `verify.flaw` diagnose problems, lines before the flaw carry `provenance` and lines after carry `trapTag = commonTrapTag`). The correct option must not carry a distractor `trapTag`, and at least one distractor must carry `commonTrapTag` with value matching `trapValue`.
     - Every choice option that declares a SymPy-evaluable `provenance` / `trapFormula` / `derivation` is evaluated from `givens`/`constants` and must equal that option's value and differ from the correct answer. When an option only has English prose `provenance` or a qualitative prose `label` (because `RawChoiceOption` in `practice-studio/src/types/drill.ts` lacks dedicated symbolic `trapFormula`/`value` fields and the vault lacks a matching `trapFormula`), `check_ai_drill_warnings`, `ai_drill_ingest` (both `--check` and ingest), and `variant_bank` (both `--check` and `--add`) report it as `"unverifiable"` (`WARNING:`).
  4. **Strict Verification Level**: All AI-written problems must carry `verificationLevel: "partial"` until verified by a blind solve.
  5. **Granular Step-by-Step Derivation Standard (00_core §7, 03_STEM §1–2, 06 §2.1)**: Every math problem must provide vertical, line-by-line derivation steps where each step represents a single mathematical transformation. Never chain multiple equalities horizontally ($A = B = C = D$). Every step must include an action-oriented `explain` string. New L2 math drills and newly added Similar variants must have ≥ 4 real steps (non-empty `latex` and `explain`, distinct across a seen set seeded with the given expression and all earlier steps using canonical SymPy/string normalisation that strips leading `=`, braces, and whitespace and collapses commutative reorderings and flipped equation sides, matching `demand_level`); existing committed drills and variants are grandfathered through the shrink-only baseline `tests/data/l2_step_floor_baseline.json` checked by `python -m math_engine.ai_drill_ingest --l2-step-gate` (rejecting duplicate IDs or malformed entries; deleting entries when fixed; adding entries is forbidden). Under-labelling is rejected across all levels (`validate_blind_solve` rejects `demand_level > level` whenever `blindSolve` is present; below L3 without `blindSolve`, `form: "reverse"` at L1, `form: "diagnose"` or `verify.flaw` below L3, and `level < arch["level"]` are rejected). Math ingest and draft `--check` flag likely math notation outside `$...$` in explanations. When `--check` reads a saved file under `drills/data` (or the configured `DRILLS_DATA_DIR`), it skips this lint and honours grandfathered L2 baseline entries.
  6. **Zero-Corrupted-LaTeX Guard (00_core §5)**: Automatically heal and reject any LaTeX escape artifact collisions (`\x0c` / `\f` formfeed, `\r` carriage return, `\x08` backspace).
  7. **No Solution in the Prompt (`check_prompt_does_not_leak_solution`)**: `prompt` states the situation, never the method. Rejected:
     - an unknown set equal to a worked computation: `using $P = 2(14) + 2(8)$`;
     - numbers re-listed by their algebraic role: `..., with coefficient $5$, constant $15$, and target value $60$`;
     - arithmetic handed over: `using $90 - 34$`, `by evaluating $(4 + 16) \div 2$`, or a second `calculate $7 \times 4 + 15$` after the real question.
     Allowed: the expression or equation that *is* the task (`Solve $5x + 15 = 60$.`, `Evaluate $2^3 \times 2^4$.`) and general formulas without substituted numbers (`$A = \pi r^2$`). Audit any unit with `python scripts/audit_solution_leaks.py [UNIT] [--display]`. Drills ingested before this gate are not re-checked.
  8. **Real-world context must be real (`validate_context_check`)**: plain prompts are the default and leave `context` empty. A story is used only for word problems or when the level needs the solver to model a situation, and then a separate reviewer (the blind solver, or its own subagent for L1–L2) judges it on three criteria:
     - **Domain law**: the operation in the problem is the law that domain really uses (series impedances add; complex power is $S = V\bar{I}$; a quantum phase factor has modulus 1). Wrong law = fail.
     - **Units and bounds**: like terms share a unit, products and quotients give the right unit, and given values and the answer are physically possible (resistance ≥ 0, a passive reflection coefficient ≤ 1 in magnitude, a probability in $[0, 1]$).
     - **Removal test**: strip the domain words. If the problem means exactly the same thing, the story is only a label. A label is ok when it is literally true (the area of a triangular gable panel is the triangle's area) and fails when it gives the number a meaning it does not have (calling $4i^2 - 9$ a phase factor, calling a product of two reactances a coupling coefficient).
     The result is stored as `contextCheck: {"verdict": "ok" | "fail", "note"}`; a non-empty `context` without it, or with `fail`, is rejected. On `fail`, fix the story or make the problem plain; never edit the verdict.
  9. **No Duplicated Choice Options in Prompt (`find_choice_prompt_option_duplicates`)**: choice prompts never list the options; options live only in `answerSpec.options` (Studio and Notion render them). *(Exception: L3 diagnose problems with `verify.flaw` show `Line N: $...$` work lines in `prompt`; `quality_rules.py` strips those lines when checking duplicates — see `rules/06_quality_contract.md` §2.3.)*
- `[code]` **Mandatory AI Contract Guard for Code (`validate_ai_code_contract`)**:
  1. **Zero Toy Code Guard (04_CODE §5.3)**: Reject `foo`, `bar`, `baz`, `test1`, `funcA`, or meaningless variables in prompt, code, and test suites. Must model realistic production domains.
  2. **Zero Wall-of-Text Guard (04_CODE §4)**: Prompt > 250 characters must be structured with Markdown sections (`**` or `###`), bullet points (`- `), and code identifiers in backticks (`` ` ``).
  3. **Zero-Leak Hint Guard (04_CODE §7)**: `inputHint` and `placeholder` must not leak answer tokens, accepted values, HTTP status codes/methods, or flawed line numbers.
  4. **Dual Pre-Execution Verification**:
     - Canonical solution (`solutionCode`) must run 100% Green on test suites.
     - Starter code (`starterCode`) for debugging problems must fail at least one reproducer test (Red before Green).
  5. **100% Local IDE Scaffolding**: `ingest_ai_code_drill` automatically materializes `drills/workspace/<drillId>/` with `package.json`, `README.md`, and sets relative `workspaceFile` (Practice Studio computes `vscodeFolderUrl` / `vscodeUrl` at runtime; no `cursor://` links).
  6. **No Duplicated Choice Options in Prompt (`find_choice_prompt_option_duplicates`)**: choice prompts never list the options; options live only in `answerSpec.options` (Studio and Notion render them).
  7. **Visible Examples & Exemption (`check_problem_examples`)**: New PRG coding drill problems (and every lab milestone in `lab_ingest`) require 2–3 normal-case LeetCode-style `examples` (`[{ input, output, explanation?, call? }]`) verified against the reference solution and free of hidden-test / `commonTrapTag` leaks. Pure `code-tracing` / `predict-output` / `trace-explain` drill problems with a fixed `codeSnippet` (or without `solutionCode`/`visibleTests`) are exempt from `examples` so they do not spoil the answer.
  8. **AnswerSpec Schema Validation (`validate_answer_spec`)**: `validate_ai_code_contract` validates `answerSpec` against `math_engine/answer_spec_registry.py` whenever `answerSpec` is present or the problem is non-coding, rejecting deprecated types (`code_output`, `line_number`, `find_bug`) and missing required fields.
- `[code]` V1 automatic: `verify_problem` (G1–G6 item gates) and `validate_ai_problem_contract` / `validate_ai_code_contract`.
- `[code]` Scope and levels: an archetype is a skill tagged with its unit (`unitCode`); a unit drill draws only on that unit's archetypes, a chapter drill on all its units' (`load_scope_archetypes`). An archetype's `level` is its base level; problems may build on it at any level from there up, and the gates decide whether the level is earned. `scripts/vault_report.py` counts, per archetype and level, ingested problems that earned it (L3+: the blind solve's mapped level) and issue-log `level` / `gate-gap` failures (accepting canonical `<SCOPE>/<key>@L<n>`, `<SCOPE>/<key> (vault)`, `<DRILL_ID>/<QID>`, and legacy `<SCOPE>/<QID>` or file-path `where` entries without rewriting `issues/log.jsonl`; unresolved scope records are listed as unattributed scope issues), and flags `FAILING@Ln` and `UNPROVEN`. Pass `--usage [--json] [--no-history]` for a read-only report of current drills and active variants against retired drills in Git history. Shallow checkouts report that history is unavailable. A `FAILING` level gets a `levelNote` on how to earn it, or the archetype stops being used there, when the issue log is cleared.
- `[code]` Vault gate (`validate_against_vault`, run by `--check` and ingest): `archetypeKey` must be in the unit's vault and `commonTrapTag` must be one of that archetype's `cognitiveTraps`, so `trapMatched` only reports misconceptions the vault defines. If no listed trap fits, add the new trap to the unit/chapter vault file first (passing vault checks) before using it in a draft; do not stop mid-run to ask the learner — log the vault addition and note it in the final report.
- `[manual]` + `[code]` V2 blind solve: a separate session receives only the prompt (plus 06 §2.3) and returns an answer, whether the prompt is ambiguous, the six demand tags of 06 §2.3 (`setup`, `strategy`, `check`, `task`, `reverse`, `steps`) and the most likely wrong answer; it does not pick a level. Compare the answer through `check_answer`. Mismatch, "ambiguous", or an answer changed mid-reply = reject or reword. For every L3+ problem the result is recorded as `blindSolve` and the vault gate maps the tags to a level (`demand_level`) and rejects the problem if that level is below `level`, if `check: edge` names nothing it ruled out (`edgeRejected`), or if the prompt bolts on a sentinel answer ("otherwise report 0"), so a direct-computation problem can never pass as L3+ (G2 counts steps, concepts, `constraints` and `category` but cannot judge demand, so a single-unit L4/L5 with a passing `blindSolve` satisfies G2; changing `category` or adding `constraints` to get past it is not allowed). One redesign at most, then drop the problem and log it (06 §2.3 stop rule). A likely wrong answer that differs from `trapValue` = re-check that the trap matches `commonTrapTag` (G4 checks the arithmetic, not the misconception). Without V1 and V2 the problem remains `verificationLevel: "partial"`, never `machine-verified`.
- `[manual]` + `[code]` V3 automated review (`validate_review`, required by ingest): the learner studies these drills, so they never preview or approve them (a preview would show the answers). Instead a separate reviewer subagent sees the whole draft (prompt, answer, steps, hints) and the brief, re-solves every problem (L1–L2 included) and checks: the answer; ambiguity (units and conventions, rounding, wording); that the problem exercises its archetype and trap; that the drill fits the brief (levels, count, plain vs. word problems, the diagnose cap, variety); and that hints help without leaking. It records `review: {"verdict": "pass" | "fix", "note"}` on each problem. On `fix`, the author fixes the problem once and the reviewer re-checks it; if it still fails, drop it and write one replacement. Every `fix` or drop is logged (`rules/00_core.md` §11). The learner reports problems found while studying the same way.
- `[code]` **Unit start & source synthesis flow**: `start-unit` runs `scripts.source_find <UNIT> --plan` (which warns on unregistered folders under `sources/` or `sources/prg/` missing from `docs/sources.md`, ignoring `_`-prefixed folders, skips `NO-AUTOMATION` and `scope`-only families, and writes `sources/_index/plan_<UNIT>.json`) between building archetypes and writing the Notion page for both `MTH-*` and `PRG-*` units; if planned batches or carried-forward cards exist, it hands off to `synthesize-source` steps 3–5 (worker-batch extraction via `scripts.extract_cards_job` + `run-worker.sh` until saturation or budget → `source_gap` → Claude vault update + reviewer).
- `[code]` **Carried-forward cards & real `laterUnit` codes**: `math_engine.source_cores.load_core_cards(unit)` also returns cards stored in other units' JSONL files whose `laterUnit == unit` or `units` contains `unit` (deduped by `id`, marked `carriedFrom`), and `scripts.source_gap` reports them as current cards. `validate_core_card` validates `laterUnit` against `id_map.json` unit/chapter codes (warns and rejects unknown codes unless prefixed `"UNMAPPED:"`); remap legacy tags with `python -m scripts.source_cores_retag <file> [--apply]`.
- `[code]` + `[manual]` **Post-ingest Similar coverage** — `ai_drill_ingest` prints a non-blocking warning for uncovered pairs. Complete the coverage checks in §6 before opening the drill PR.

## 6. Variant bank
- `[code]` **Storage & schema:** Math variant banks live at `drills/variants/<UNIT_CODE>.json` as `{ "unitCode": "<UNIT>", "variants": [ ... ] }`. Each entry is a full math problem object in the same schema as drill problems (`prompt`, `answerSpec`, `steps` with `explain`, `verify`, `trapValue`, `commonTrapTag`, `archetypeKey`, `level`, `display`/`plainText` rules including omitting `plainText` on `choice` problems, and figure fields) plus `variantId` (`VAR-<UNIT>-<archetypeKey>-NN`) and `sourceArchetype` (= `archetypeKey`). Bank files hold content only — never learner state — and are never synced to Notion.
- `[code]` **File containment:** Resolve unit-code bank selections as regular JSON files within the active variants directory. Reject symlinked bank entries for reads and writes.
- `[code]` **Gates (`python -m math_engine.variant_bank --check <file|UNIT>`):** Runs every single-problem gate from `math_engine.ai_drill_ingest` (`validate_against_vault`, `validate_review` when present, figure decisions/autofill, `validate_ai_problem_contract`, `verify_problem` G1–G6 & zero-leak/solution-leak checks) plus uniqueness checks: every variant must differ from all existing drill problems in the unit scope and every other variant of the same archetype (different prompt/numbers/context and a different canonical answer, never a copy). Both `ai_drill_ingest --check` and `variant_bank --check` state in their final gate summary how many drills/variants were checked vs skipped by the originality gate and why (and `python -m math_engine.ai_drill_ingest --originality-summary [--json]` summarizes skips across all canonical math drills and variant banks). `python -m math_engine.variant_bank --orphans [--json]` (and the read-only summary in `--coverage-gate`) lists orphaned variant banks with no matching active drill without deleting files.
- `[code]` **Authoring & coverage:**
  - `python -m math_engine.variant_bank --coverage <UNIT|drill.json>` reports variant counts per `(archetypeKey, level)` and flags pairs with fewer than three.
  - CI enforces `python -m math_engine.variant_bank --coverage-gate` for drills in `drills/data/MTH-*/`. Every pair in a new math drill needs at least three variants. The gate rejects uncovered pairs absent from `tests/data/variant_coverage_baseline.json` and stale baseline pairs that are covered or no longer used. Delete the exact pair reported and remove the drill key if its list becomes empty. Adding baseline entries is forbidden. PRG is outside the scope of the gate.
  - Create enough AI-written variants at the exact level to bring each pair to three. Use the blind-solve, reviewer, and originality gates and append via `python -m math_engine.variant_bank --add <draft.json>` (`add_variants` enforces `validate_review` with `review: {verdict: "pass", note}` on newly added variants, while `check_variant_bank` preserves compatibility with existing committed banks).
- `[code]` **Quarantined legacy variants:** Legacy variants that predate current gates (such as the 33 variants in `MTH-T02-C02-U05`, `MTH-T02-C03-U03`, and `MTH-T02-C04-U05` after PR #338) carry `"status": "quarantined"`, `"quarantineReason"`, and `"since"` while preserving content intact. Studio loaders, Python selectors (`variant_bank.find_variant`, `_load_scope_variants`, `drill_review`), and `--coverage-gate` skip quarantined variants completely. `scripts.vault_report <UNIT>` prints `Quarantined variants: N` when present. Re-verify with `python -m math_engine.variant_bank --reverify <UNIT>` (runs current distractor-provenance, review, L2 step-floor, answer-check, uniqueness, and originality gates). `--apply` may restore passing items in a PR only when originality runs with a located source index and cached pages. Ordinary variant checks and drill-quality reports exclude quarantined items, and the originality and orphan reports count them separately.
- `[code]` **Studio serving & variant isolation:** `GET /api/variant?drillId=&qid=&mode=` accepts `mode=list|open|example`: it lists current-cycle opened tab summaries and per-cycle exhaustion, resumes an opened tab, or returns an eligible worked example. Listing performs no allocation; after Reset, prior-cycle variant history stays in `state.variants`, while `mode=list` shows no tabs until `+` is pressed. After `similarUnlock`, `POST /api/variant` with `action: "open-next"` serves the next matching variant for the current cycle in existing bank order (preferring variants never served in earlier cycles, then wrapping from the first) and returns the new tab, or returns `exhausted: true` once every matching variant has been served in the current cycle. Opened variants use an opaque `variantToken`. The same endpoint accepts token-scoped `skip`, `reveal-steps`, `reset`, `duration`, `self-score`, `toggle-mistake`, `diverged-step`, and `note` actions. Original practice cycles are managed through `POST /api/reveal`; checks go through `POST /api/check-answer` and reveals through `/api/reveal`, both using `variantToken`. Do not send or document `variantId` as an API credential. Hint/display may be revealed before a substantive attempt; steps unlock after a substantive check or Skip (with `reveal-steps` kept server-compatible). Variant checks, skips, self-scores, tags, and notes stay isolated from the original problem's score and `review_items.json`; they do not change the original review item. See `practice-studio/src/app/api/variant/route.ts`, `practice-studio/src/app/api/check-answer/route.ts`, `practice-studio/src/app/api/reveal/route.ts`, and `docs/studio_design.md`.
